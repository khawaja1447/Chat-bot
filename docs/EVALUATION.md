# Retrieval Evaluation
_Generated by `python eval/run_eval.py --write`. Do not edit by hand._

**Corpus:** 6 documents, 29 pages, 58 chunks.  
**Questions:** 109, each labelled with the document and page that actually contains the answer (`eval/golden_set.yaml`).

Retrieval is measured on its own — no LLM call, no API key, deterministic. If the right passage never reaches the prompt, prompt engineering cannot recover it.

## Metrics
- **Recall@k** — share of questions with at least one correct chunk in the top k.
- **MRR@10** — mean of 1/rank of the first correct chunk; rewards ranking it first.

## Retrieval strategies

| Configuration | Recall@1 | Recall@3 | Recall@5 | MRR@10 | p50 latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| dense-only | 0.661 | 0.862 | 0.927 | 0.774 | 9 ms |
| hybrid | 0.743 | 0.945 | 0.972 | 0.844 | 9 ms |
| dense+rerank | 0.927 | 0.982 | 0.991 | 0.958 | 137 ms |
| hybrid+rerank | 0.908 | 0.972 | 0.982 | 0.942 | 136 ms |

**dense+rerank** scores highest: Recall@1 **+40.3%**, Recall@5 **+6.9%**, MRR **+23.8%** against the dense-only baseline, for about +128 ms of median latency.

Recall@1 is the metric to watch. Recall@5 saturates on a corpus this size — five chunks out of a few dozen is a large share of it — so a high Recall@5 here says more about the corpus than about the retriever.
